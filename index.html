Financial Analysis Website

Financial calculations, made readable

This site takes stock and portfolio data and turns it into the numbers investors use to judge risk and performance.

Enter prices or returns for the securities you want to study, pick a time period, and the site calculates the results for you. It is meant to make common finance measures easy to try and easy to understand.

AI Project: Financial Analysis Website

This website is a class project. I built it with the help of an AI tool (Claude) to practice applying finance concepts in a working web application.

The goal is a site with clearly labeled sections that performs real financial calculations, including measures of risk, return, and portfolio performance. The calculations live in the Financial Analysis tab.

The example data is a five-year history of monthly returns for Apple (AAPL) and the S&P 500 (SPY), from December 2021 through September 2026. Returns are calculated from adjusted closing prices, which include dividends.

Total return

Your gain or loss, as a percent of what you paid. Also called the holding-period return.

Formula and math

HPR = (Ending price − Beginning price + Dividends) ÷ Beginning price

Yearly return

The steady yearly rate that would produce the same total. Also called the annualized return.

Formula and math

Annualized = (1 + HPR)1 ÷ Years − 1

Volatility

How bumpy the ride is. Higher means bigger ups and downs.

Formula and math

σ = √[ Σ(rᵢ − mean)² ÷ (n − 1) ], annualized σ × √(periods per year)

Sharpe ratio

How much extra return you earned for each unit of risk.

Formula and math

Sharpe = (Annualized return − Risk-free rate) ÷ Annualized volatility

Beta

How strongly the stock reacts when the market moves.

Formula and math

β = Covariance(stock, market) ÷ Variance(market)

Correlation

How closely the stock moves with the market, from −1 to +1.

Formula and math

ρ = Covariance(stock, market) ÷ (σ stock × σ market)

Alpha (CAPM)

Whether the stock earned more or less than its risk predicted. Shown per year.

Formula and math

α = Stock return − [Risk-free rate + β × (Market return − Risk-free rate)]

Yearly returns are the average return per period times the number of periods in a year. Volatility uses the sample standard deviation (dividing by n − 1).

What if I split my money between two stocks?

Enter each stock's return and how much of your money goes into Stock A. The rest goes into Stock B. The result updates as you type.

Filled in for you. The two always add to 100%.

Portfolio return

The return on your combined money.

Formula and math

Portfolio return = (Weight A × Return A) + (Weight B × Return B)

Get returns for the calculators

  1. Enter a ticker above and open its price history.
  2. Choose a monthly frequency and a date range, such as the last five years.
  3. Copy the adjusted closing prices, which include dividends.
  4. On the Financial Analysis tab, open Risk and performance, then use the price converter under the calculator to turn them into returns.

This site does not pull live prices in by itself. The sites above are the source of the live numbers, and you bring them here to analyze.
